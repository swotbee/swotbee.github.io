Cedar Vale Foods buys solar support and battery monitoring from Beacon Grid. One customer, two services. Where should each agreement's terms, sales history and billing evidence live in HubSpot?

We'll follow solar support, compare battery monitoring, and build a map you can use on your own records. The HubSpot screens show prepared drafts. The documents are illustrations, not signed agreements.

Solar support runs from November twenty twenty-six through October twenty twenty-seven. Battery monitoring starts and ends a month later. Each has its own service scope and renewal decision.

That's why we give them separate agreement identities. It isn't a rule of one Contract per product. Services managed under one set of terms can share a Contract with several line items.

The Company identifies the customer. It cannot, by itself, tell you which service an end date or a price belongs to. Keep those facts connected to the specific agreement.

Start with the legal evidence. The master agreement, service schedule and accepted amendments establish what the parties agreed. HubSpot's commercial Contract does not replace those documents.

Next, the Deal answers: which sales opportunity are we working on? Here, the solar draft links to its source Deal. A future renewal opportunity has a different job from the original sale.

A Quote presents a proposal for buyer acceptance, when that route is used. Accepted HubSpot quotes can create Contracts. An agreement signed elsewhere can instead be recorded through the direct-create route, when available.

The commercial Contract holds the services, term and committed revenue being managed. This solar example is still a draft, so its values are proposed. A populated record is not proof of customer acceptance.

The invoice answers what was billed. The payment record shows payment activity and status. For this example, HubSpot billing stays off. We have not created invoices or demonstrated collections.

A Subscription needs one extra check. In the standard billing flow, it can manage recurring billing. In a connected billing setup, it can support reporting while the Contract controls billing. Check which setup your account uses before deciding where to make changes.

Now check the numbers. The solar line is one thousand US dollars per month, quantity one, for twelve payments. That gives twelve thousand dollars across this fixed term.

Those numbers describe different measures. The monthly recurring price is one thousand dollars. The term total is twelve thousand. Neither tells you how much has been invoiced or paid.

If a fifteen-hundred-dollar commissioning fee belongs to the same agreement, it adds to the total commitment, not the recurring monthly price. If it is separate, leave it outside the solar agreement. Don't carry a one-time fee into recurring renewal charges.

Always name the field you're comparing. Deal Amount, recurring value and Contract total can have different calculation rules. Check the line items and term that produced the displayed number.

Battery monitoring follows the same responsibility map. It's two hundred and fifty dollars monthly for twelve payments: three thousand dollars across its term. Its end date is November thirtieth, twenty twenty-seven.

The customer is shared, but the evidence, terms and next action must point to battery monitoring. A link through the same Company is not enough to prove that an invoice or a Deal belongs to that agreement.

Associations also don't guarantee synchronization. HubSpot can copy line items into separate record instances. Compare their values and parent records, rather than assuming one edit updates every linked record.
