Cedar Vale signs up for three years of service. The price is agreed from the start: twelve thousand dollars in year one, thirteen thousand two hundred in year two, and fourteen thousand five hundred and twenty in year three.

How should HubSpot represent that? Start with the source agreement, its dated price schedule and the billing arrangement you intend to use. Each anniversary moves to the next agreed price; it is not automatically a new sale or a renewal negotiation.

In this fictional example, the commitment runs from January twenty twenty-seven through December twenty twenty-nine. Each price applies for exactly one year. The total across the three years is thirty-nine thousand seven hundred and twenty dollars.

The contract term tells us how long the commitment lasts. The price schedule tells us which rate applies when. Billing frequency answers a separate question: how often do we issue an invoice?

With annual billing, the example has one charge of twelve thousand dollars in year one, then thirteen thousand two hundred, then fourteen thousand five hundred and twenty. Invoice issue and due dates still follow the agreed billing terms.

With monthly billing, year one has twelve charges of one thousand dollars. Year two has twelve of eleven hundred. Year three has twelve of twelve hundred and ten. Both patterns cover the same three-year price schedule.

Keep the source agreement and its price schedule identifiable. The commercial Contract represents the commitment being managed. The Deal records the sales opportunity, and a Quote presents the proposal when that route is used.

Represent each period with its own start, finite term, quantity and applicable price. The three phases are sequential. If all three prices start together, you have described concurrent charges instead of a yearly increase.

Keep the full commitment separate from the current period's rate. Thirty-nine thousand seven hundred and twenty dollars is the three-year total. It is not this year's recurring rate or evidence of cash collected.

HubSpot now documents ramp pricing in Revenue Hub quotes: add the product as separate line items, set each phase's billing start date, and give each phase its intended term. This is a documented option, not a completed setup demonstration.

That quote route requires Revenue Hub Professional or Enterprise and a Revenue Hub seat. The documentation also states a restriction on ramp pricing with HubSpot payments. Verify the processor and account path before promising automated billing.

A direct Contract and a billing migration are separate setup routes. Check which one your account supports and how it handles dated line items. A migration ramp key belongs to its migration process; it is not a field every quote needs. Choose the route before copying the schedule into HubSpot.

If Finance bills elsewhere, keep that system responsible for its invoice schedule and check the handoff. Recording three prices in HubSpot alone does not configure the external billing system.

Now test the boundary, not just the total. On December thirty-first, twenty twenty-seven, the example is still in year one. From January first, twenty twenty-eight, year two's price applies. There should be no overlap and no missing service period.

For the monthly pattern, the expected rate changes from one thousand to eleven hundred dollars. The old phase must stop before the new one charges. Adding both would produce twenty-one hundred dollars for the same month.

Repeat the check at the next anniversary, when the monthly rate becomes twelve hundred and ten dollars. For annual billing, check the corresponding annual amount instead. These are expected results; no invoices or automatic transitions were executed here.

At the end of year three, confirm what the agreement actually says. Do not invent a fourth-year price or assume automatic renewal. Keep the source terms, commercial records and billing schedule aligned.

Reconcile the complete schedule. Twelve thousand, plus thirteen thousand two hundred, plus fourteen thousand five hundred and twenty equals thirty-nine thousand seven hundred and twenty dollars. Thirty-six monthly charges produce the same total before taxes and other agreed adjustments.
