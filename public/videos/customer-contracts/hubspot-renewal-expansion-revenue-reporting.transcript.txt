Calling the entire purchase new sales overstates growth. Calling everything renewal hides expansion. The answer starts by classifying the components, then reconciling the customer total separately from the change in recurring value.

The twelve-thousand-dollar renewal retains the existing recurring service. It contributes twelve thousand dollars to the new recurring total, but zero incremental growth. We are carrying the baseline forward, not creating that baseline again.

The additional service adds three thousand dollars per year. That is recurring expansion in this example. Compare the products and entitlements with the prior agreement so that an existing charge is not accidentally labelled as a new service.

The fifteen-hundred-dollar commissioning fee belongs in the purchase reconciliation, but not in annual recurring revenue. Its invoice and commission treatment can follow separate rules. A payment being collected today does not make it recurring.

For recurring value, start with the twelve-thousand-dollar opening baseline. There is no contraction or churn in this case. Add three thousand dollars of expansion. Closing annual recurring revenue is fifteen thousand dollars, and recurring growth is three thousand.

For the one-year purchase, add twelve thousand renewal, three thousand expansion, and fifteen hundred one-time work. That totals sixteen thousand five hundred dollars. Keep this consideration total separate from closing ARR, invoice cash, and recognized revenue.
