## 1. Definition & Core Concepts

### What Is a Multi-Tiered Affiliate Incentive Structure?

A multi-tiered affiliate incentive structure is a compensation model where affiliates (or salespeople) earn commissions on multiple levels:

- **Tier 1 (Direct):** Commission on their own sales
- **Tier 2 (Sub-affiliate):** Commission on sales made by affiliates they recruit
- **Tier 3+ (Network):** Commission on sales made by recruits of their recruits

This creates a **cascading earning structure** where each participant benefits from the performance of their downstream network.

### How It Works — Simple Example

```
Mike (Tier 1 Affiliate)
├── Earns 10% on his own sales
├── Jenny (Recruited by Mike — Tier 2)
│   ├── Earns 10% on her own sales
│   └── Mike earns 5% on Jenny's sales
└── Taylor (Recruited by Jenny — Tier 3)
    ├── Earns 10% on her own sales
    ├── Jenny earns 5% on Taylor's sales
    └── Mike earns 2% on Taylor's sales
```

If Taylor closes a $10,000 deal:
- Taylor earns $1,000 (10%)
- Jenny earns $500 (5%)
- Mike earns $200 (2%)
- **Total commission outlay: $1,700 (17%)**

### Key Components

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Tiers** | Number of levels in the program (typically 2-3; up to 5 in some programs) |
| **Commission Rates** | Percentage earned at each tier (descending from Tier 1 to Tier N) |
| **Attribution Model** | Rules for who gets credit for a sale |
| **Qualification Criteria** | Minimum requirements to advance tiers or earn commissions |
| **Clawback Rules** | When and how commissions are reversed (cancellations, refunds) |
| **Payout Schedule** | When commissions are paid (monthly, bi-weekly, upon invoice payment) |

## 2. Multi-Tier vs. Standard vs. MLM

### Comparison Table

| Aspect | Standard Affiliate | Multi-Tier Affiliate | MLM |
|--------|-------------------|---------------------|-----|
| Earning source | Own sales only | Own sales + sub-affiliate sales | Own sales + downline sales + recruitment bonuses |
| Recruitment incentive | None | Yes (earn from recruits) | Primary driver |
| Typical tiers | 1 | 2-3 | 5-10+ |
| Product focus | High | High | Sometimes secondary to recruitment |
| Legal risk | None | Low (if product-focused) | Higher (FTC scrutiny) |
| Upfront cost to join | None | None | Often requires inventory purchase |
| Income source | Product/service sales | Product/service sales | Mix of sales and recruitment fees |

### Legal Distinction (FTC Guidance)

Per the [Federal Trade Commission](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/business-guidance-concerning-multi-level-marketing):

> **Multi-tier affiliate programs are legal** as long as:
> - Compensation is primarily based on **actual product/service sales to real customers**
> - Affiliates are **not required to purchase inventory** or pay fees to participate
> - The program does **not primarily reward recruitment** over sales

**Where it becomes illegal:** When the compensation structure primarily rewards recruiting new members rather than actual sales from real customers. The key test is whether participants can earn meaningful income from product sales alone, without recruiting.

**Best practice for multi-tier programs:**
- Keep tiers to 2-3 maximum
- Ensure commission rates are tied to verifiable product sales
- Don't require affiliates to purchase products themselves
- Document that the majority of revenue comes from external customers

## 3. Commission Models & Attribution

### 5 Affiliate Commission Structures

| Model | How It Works | Best For | Pros | Cons |
|-------|-------------|----------|------|------|
| **Tiered** | Commission rate increases as sales volume grows | Large programs seeking scalable growth | Motivates top performers; rewards loyalty | Complex to track; may discourage new affiliates |
| **Performance-Based** | Pay tied to specific KPIs (leads, conversions, revenue) | B2B/SaaS lead generation | Pay only for results; tracks ROI | Requires accurate tracking; higher admin costs |
| **Time-Limited** | Elevated commissions during specific periods | Seasonal campaigns, product launches | Creates urgency; boosts engagement | Short-term focus reduces retention |
| **Product-Specific** | Different rates per product/category | E-commerce with multiple products | Prioritizes high-margin items | Requires constant updates |
| **Hybrid** | Combines multiple models | Enterprise networks with diverse partnerships | Maximum flexibility | Most complex to manage |

*Source: [impact.com](https://impact.com/affiliate/5-effective-affiliate-commission-structures/)*

### Attribution Models for Multi-Tier Programs

| Model | How Credit Is Assigned | Best For | Pros | Cons |
|-------|----------------------|----------|------|------|
| **First-Touch** | First affiliate to engage the customer gets 100% credit | Content creators, long sales cycles | Rewards discovery and awareness; protects content affiliates from coupon poachers | Ignores nurturing and closing efforts |
| **Last-Touch** | Last affiliate before conversion gets 100% credit | Bottom-funnel optimization | Simple to implement; optimizes for conversions | Undervalues awareness and consideration stages |
| **Linear** | Equal credit split among all affiliates in the journey | Fairness-focused programs | Simple, fair distribution | Doesn't reward any specific contribution |
| **Time-Decay** | More credit to touchpoints closer to conversion | Long B2B sales cycles | Rewards recent, relevant interactions | Complex to implement; can undervalue discovery |
| **Position-Based** | 40% first, 40% last, 20% split among middle touches | B2B SaaS with multiple stakeholders | Balances awareness and conversion credit | Requires multi-touch tracking infrastructure |

**Real-world examples:**
- **HubSpot Affiliate Program** and **Shopify Partners** use **first-click attribution** — rewarding customer acquisition efforts, especially for longer sales cycles (30+ days)
- Most DTC brands use **last-click** for simplicity
- Enterprise B2B programs increasingly adopting **position-based** models

### Accelerator & Decelerator Best Practices

| Best Practice | Detail |
|---------------|--------|
| **Pair accelerators with decelerators** | If you don't have accelerators, decelerators are useless. Reps see decelerator-only plans as unfair |
| **Limit tiers to 3-4** | More than 4 tiers is hard for reps to remember, losing the motivational effect |
| **Tier 3 should be rare** | Ideally no more than 10% of reps reach Tier 3; Tiers 1+2 combined should be under 25% |
| **Avoid overly generous multipliers** | A 3x multiplier creates "lumpiness" (200% one quarter, 50% the next). Use 1.25x–1.5x for consistency |
| **Review every 6-12 months** | Commission structures aren't permanent; adapt to market and team changes |

## 5. Implementing in HubSpot

### The Problem

HubSpot has **no native commission tracking** or tiered commission calculation. Users must build workarounds using calculated properties, workflows, and custom objects — or use third-party tools.

### Approach 1: Calculated Properties (Basic Tiers)

**Step 1: Create Custom Properties**

| Property Name | Type | Purpose |
|---------------|------|---------|
| Commission Threshold 1 | Currency (view-only) | Tier 1 cap (e.g., $15,000) |
| Commission Threshold 2 | Currency (view-only) | Tier 2 cap (e.g., $50,000) |
| Commission Calc Level 2 Adjust | Currency | Revenue amount in Tier 2 range |
| Commission Calc Level 3 Adjust | Currency | Revenue amount in Tier 3 range |
| Commission Earned | Calculated (equation) | Final commission amount |

**Step 2: Build the Formula**

For a 3-tier structure (5% up to $15K, 4% for $15K-$50K, 3% above $50K):

```
IF( deal_amount <= 15000,
    deal_amount * 0.05,
    IF( deal_amount <= 50000,
        (15000 * 0.05) + ((deal_amount - 15000) * 0.04),
        (15000 * 0.05) + (35000 * 0.04) + ((deal_amount - 50000) * 0.03)
    )
)
```

**Example:** A $60,000 deal earns:
- Tier 1: $15,000 × 5% = $750
- Tier 2: $35,000 × 4% = $1,400
- Tier 3: $10,000 × 3% = $300
- **Total commission: $2,450**

**Step 3: Create a Workflow**
- Trigger: `deal_amount is known`
- Re-enrollment: ON (so commission recalculates when deal value changes)
- Actions: Set the tier-level adjustment properties to correct revenue amounts

### Approach 2: Custom Report Builder (Accelerators)

For **quota-based accelerators** (where commission rate depends on cumulative performance, not individual deal size):

1. Build a report grouped by **Deal Owner** and **Close Date (Year)**
2. Use a formula field with IF logic to apply tiers based on aggregated closed-won amount
3. This calculates commission per owner per time period in the report layer

**Limitation:** Report-level calculations don't write back to deal records.

### Approach 3: Custom Objects (Advanced)

For Enterprise HubSpot users:
1. Create a **Commission** custom object
2. Workflow creates a commission record each time a deal closes
3. Commission object stores: deal amount, commission rate, payout status, rep name
4. Finance team uses commission object reports instead of exporting deals monthly

### Approach 4: Operations Hub + Custom Code

For complex scenarios (scaled percentages, variable rates per rep, split commissions):
- Use Operations Hub **custom code actions** in workflows
- Write JavaScript/Python to calculate tiered commissions with any logic
- Write results back to custom properties

## 8. Best Practices & Common Pitfalls

### Best Practices

| # | Practice | Detail |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | **Keep tiers to 2-3 maximum** | Most operators succeed with 2 tiers. A 3rd tier works only with small overrides and strict quality gates. More than 3 adds complexity and margin risk without growth |
| 2 | **Use descending commission rates** | Tier 1 highest (10-20%), Tier 2 moderate (3-5%), Tier 3 minimal (1-2%). Maintains profitability while incentivizing recruitment |
| 3 | **Add quality gates** | Minimum sales requirements before sub-affiliate commissions activate. Prevents bonus abuse and ensures recruits are productive |
| 4 | **Pair accelerators with decelerators** | A plan with only decelerators feels unfair. Accelerators "pay for" decelerators and create balanced motivation |
| 5 | **Limit Tier 3 access** | No more than 10% of affiliates should reach the top tier. If too many reach it, your rates are too generous |
| 6 | **Provide real-time dashboards** | Affiliates are 3x more engaged when they can see earnings in real-time vs. monthly statements |
| 7 | **Use first-touch attribution for content affiliates** | Protects content creators from having their referrals stolen by last-minute coupon sites |
| 8 | **Communicate clearly** | Document tier criteria, rates, and payout schedules transparently. Ambiguity kills trust |
| 9 | **Foster mentorship** | Structure incentivizes experienced affiliates to mentor recruits (their earnings depend on recruits' success) |
| 10 | **Review every 6-12 months** | Market conditions, product margins, and team composition change. Commission structures must adapt |

### Common Pitfalls

| # | Pitfall | Impact | Solution |
|---|---------|--------|----------|
| 1 | **Too many tiers** | Complexity, margin leaks, "who owns what" disputes | Limit to 2-3 tiers maximum |
| 2 | **No quality gates** | Attracts recruitment-only affiliates who never sell | Require minimum sales before sub-affiliate commissions activate |
| 3 | **Overly generous multipliers** | Lumpiness (200% one quarter, 50% next); unpredictable costs | Use 1.25x-1.5x instead of 3x multipliers |
| 4 | **CPA farming** | Sub-affiliates generate fake leads for CPA bounties | Add verification gates and clawback rules |
| 5 | **Margin leaks** | Cascading commissions eat too much of each sale | Model total commission payout per sale across all tiers before launching |
| 6 | **Pyramid scheme perception** | Legitimate programs look like MLMs to prospects | Emphasize product sales; keep tier depth minimal; document compliance |
| 7 | **Attribution disputes** | Multi-tier makes "who gets credit" more expensive | Choose clear attribution model upfront; communicate it in affiliate agreement |
| 8 | **Manual tracking** | Spreadsheets break with multi-tier complexity | Use dedicated affiliate software with built-in multi-tier support |
| 9 | **Ignoring clawbacks** | Affiliates game refund windows for commission | Implement automated clawback with appropriate cooling period |
| 10 | **No performance data** | Can't optimize what you can't measure | Require real-time analytics from day one; track per-tier conversion rates |
